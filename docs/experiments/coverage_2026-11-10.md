# More training proteins, fixed ridge rule

Status: completed by the user and verified on CPU on 10 November 2026.
This study follows the completed regularization-only experiment.

## Question and motivation

Does broader training-protein coverage improve stability prediction and stable
candidate selection on the **same** held-out proteins? Strong regularization
[reduced error but harmed selection](regularization_2026-11-10.md#completed-results--10-november-2026).
The user requested keeping that intervention separate from increasing data.
Use the original ridge penalty rule as a ranking-useful development comparator;
it is not already a validated reward for broad generation.

## Keep regularization comparable

The original solver uses **summed**, not averaged, squared prediction error.
Keeping its raw penalty at 1000 while nearly doubling training rows would weaken
regularization relative to the average data loss. Instead fix that relative
strength at the original value:

`penalty(N) = 1000 × N / 2860`

Here `N` is the number of training sequences in the current stage; `2860` is the
original training count, and `1000` is the original ridge penalty. The resulting
raw penalties are 1000, 1454.54545 and 1954.54545. They are determined by counts,
not tuned or selected on outcomes. Dividing the existing objective by `N` gives
the same regularization coefficient `1000/2860` at every stage. This is our
algebraic control, not a newly optimized stronger-regularization setting.

Feature/label normalization is recalculated from each stage's training rows only,
as in the original preprocessing. Thus the strength is fixed in each training
set's standardized coordinates; its physical interpretation is not guaranteed
identical as the population changes. The exact same primal/dual solver is reused.
The 44-parent refit must reproduce the original saved ridge's validation
predictions within 1e-4 kcal/mol or execution fails rather than comparing changed
baselines. There is no hyperparameter sweep, model-family choice or CV selection.

## Execution and metrics

Reuse all original frozen ProGen2 features. Extract mean-pooled float32 features
only for the 2,730 added training sequences, in equal-length batches of eight with
original checkpoint/tokenizer hashes verified. Release the backbone before the
three ridge fits. Each stage's constant baseline predicts that stage's training
mean, which can change as protein coverage grows. No ProGen unfreezing, MLP fitting,
GFlowNet training, new sampling space or generation.

Save all three checkpoints before reporting validation results. No validation
labels enter training or select a winning stage. All stages are reported. Test
labels are not evaluated; held-out identities remain fixed for later use.
Outer validation has already informed development, so this is not independent
confirmation.

Primary design metric: mean within-parent measured stability lift from selecting
the highest-predicted seven of 65 candidates versus uniform selection of those
same 65. Also report each parent's lift, pooled/parent-balanced prediction errors,
within-parent ranking, reference-only metrics and the decomposition into parent
mean errors and centered variant errors. Lift is not relative to ProGen sampling.
Lower RMSE without better selection is not a sufficient reward-model success.
Both improvement and deterioration must be retained; do not discard difficult
held-out parents or select additions based on evaluation performance.

This is still an assay-specific short-protein development benchmark, not proof
of new stable-protein generation. Filters and dataset provenance are inherited
from the [multi-protein protocol](multiprotein_2026-11-10.md), which cites the
[MegaScale release](https://zenodo.org/records/7992926) and original paper.

## Completed results — 10 November 2026

runs/coverage-v1 completed in **32.321 seconds**, including 31.959 seconds loading
ProGen and extracting the 2,730 added sequence features (360 forward calls,
160,290 input tokens). The original 44-parent baseline reproduces within 1.44e-6
kcal/mol. All stages share the same ten validation proteins and 650 candidate
sequences, so the stage comparisons do not change evaluation support. Test
measurements were not evaluated.

| Training parents | Ridge RMSE | Constant RMSE | Within-parent Spearman | Mean selection lift | Positive-lift validation parents |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 44 | 1.638994 | 1.447310 | 0.2854 | +0.29065 | 7/10 |
| 64 | 1.674889 | 1.4484 | 0.3291 | +0.37826 | 9/10 |
| 86 | 1.587498 | 1.4467 | 0.3195 | +0.31406 | 8/10 |

RMSE and lift are kcal/mol. The unchanged selection metric chooses seven of each
protein's 65 candidates by prediction, then compares their measured mean with
uniform selection from that same set. Stage 64 improves average lift by 0.08760
over baseline; stage 86 by 0.02340. Both improve lift for 6/10 individual proteins
relative to baseline. More positive-lift parents than baseline does not mean every
parent improved relative to its earlier lift.

**More training coverage gives modest, non-monotonic selection benefits; it does
not fix absolute stability prediction.** The 86-parent model reduces RMSE by
0.05150 versus baseline, while 64 parents worsen it. All stages still have worse
RMSE than their constant controls. Do not declare 64 a general optimum from this
already inspected validation panel or automatically promote a model to generation.

| Training parents | Parent-mean MSE | Centered-variant MSE |
|---|---:|---:|
| 44 | 1.72869 | 0.95761 |
| 64 | 1.87088 | 0.93437 |
| 86 | 1.57345 | 0.94670 |

The earlier tension persists: 64 parents improve variant prediction while making
parent-mean errors worse; 86 reduce parent-mean errors, but remain unreliable.
This is a diagnostic decomposition, not a correction available at generation time.

Post-hoc per-parent sensitivity: 2M0Y.pdb's lift increases by 0.65853 at 64 parents
and 0.58916 at 86. It accounts for roughly 75% of the net 64-parent average gain
and more than the net 86-parent gain. Without that parent, mean gain relative to
baseline would be about +0.0247 and -0.0395 respectively. This calculation is a
fragility check, not a justification for excluding a protein or recomputing the
official result on a favorable subset. Ten validation parents and one nested
training expansion do not establish statistical significance or a universal
benefit of sample count.

### Interpretation and next proposed decision

Retain all three stages as evidence. The data-size hypothesis received limited
support for local candidate ranking, but not a reliable broad reward. Before
choosing a training budget or a generation reward, assess whether the gain repeats
across other held-out protein groups with a prespecified grouped coverage
comparison and the same per-example penalty rule. Such resampling would be
developmental robustness evidence, not fresh confirmation; it should keep
evaluation candidates identical across training sizes within each comparison.
It has not been implemented or run. No new GFlowNet training follows automatically
from these results, and the stronger-regularization model remains rejected as a
reward on selection grounds.
