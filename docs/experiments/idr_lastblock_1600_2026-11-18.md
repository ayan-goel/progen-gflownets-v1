# Last-block GFlowNet: 1,600 versus 800 updates — 18 November 2026

## Question and evidence

Does one further doubling of the working training schedule materially improve
IDR charge/disorder generation? The user authorized this comparison after the
[800-update result](idr_lastblock_budget_2026-11-17.md#completed-results--18-november-2026)
and [late-learning analysis](idr_lastblock_budget_2026-11-17.md#post-hoc-late-learning-analysis--18-november-2026).
Mean final success increased from 21.78% to 29.64%. Fresh adapted-policy training
draws improved in both final 200-update windows, but final 100-update trends were
mixed and violations flattened. More learning is plausible, not guaranteed.

**Status:** completed by the user at `runs/idr-lastblock-1600-v1` after the storage
interruption recorded below. Joint success improves modestly, but both repeats
miss the predefined five-point gain threshold. The user subsequently chose to keep
1,600 as the working budget and defer further budget tests; this does not revise
the recorded gate outcome. [Results](#completed-results--18-november-2026)
and [verification](../verification.md#1600-update-result-audit--18-november-2026)
record the outcome; the [runbook](../runbook.md#1600-versus-800-updates--completed)
owns reproduction and recovery commands. No real model/scorer or GPU was executed
by the assistant during analysis.

This is a single bounded test motivated by a material preceding gain, not an
open-ended search. Reward changes, more unfrozen layers, another property and
new baselines are separate alternatives; they would obscure this training-budget
question. No new literature claim motivates the doubling: it is an engineering
choice grounded in the saved results above.

## Fixed method and controlled change

Reuse the unchanged [last-block trainer](idr_lastblock_2026-11-16.md) and
[800-update comparison conventions](idr_lastblock_budget_2026-11-17.md).
Start each new fit from the original supervised head and original ProGen weights.
Train a copied final transformer block plus the GFN head with ordinary trajectory
balance (TB). The original ProGen reward reference remains frozen. Earlier blocks,
embeddings, final normalization and LM output head also remain frozen.

| Setting | Choice and rationale |
|---|---|
| Updates per trained arm | 1,600; exactly twice the completed 800 budget |
| Learning-rate decay | Tenfold after update 1,200; preserve the existing 75%-of-run convention |
| Initial rates | Head `1e-4`, direct task branch `1e-3`, normalizer `1e-2`, copied block `1e-5`; unchanged |
| Optimizer and gradients | Adam, zero weight decay, microbatch four, separate norm-one clipping; unchanged |
| Reward | Continuous acceptance reward, penalty 2.5; unchanged |
| Sequence task | Eight development prompts, 16 supplied residues + 64 generated residues, 20 canonical choices, temperature one, float32; unchanged |
| Data collection | Every four updates: eight frozen-policy, eight adapted-policy, eight prior and eight fixed-bank replay paths; unchanged |

Both frozen-head and adapted arms consume the same 32 trajectories per update.
The frozen arm is retained because it supplies part of the online mixture. Fresh
successes do not enter the fixed replay bank. Inherit diagnostic exclusions and
exposure tracking. This changes the training budget **and** absolute decay point,
not the reward, model capacity or objective.

| Repeat | Training seed | Replay seed | New evaluation seed |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| `replicate-1` | 223000 | 225000 | 633000 |
| `replicate-2` | 323000 | 325000 | 733000 |

Training/replay streams match the completed 800-update fits; evaluation streams
are new and checked against preceding streams. Schedules diverge at update 601,
so later trajectories need not match. The new update-800 checkpoint cannot replace
the original 800-update control. Saved checkpoints lack optimizer/RNG state, so
the experiment restarts from the original initialization rather than pretending
to resume exactly.

## Cost and evaluation

Per repeat: 400 refresh rounds, **9,600 fresh training trajectories**, 3,200 replay
draws and **51,200 trajectory presentations per trained arm**. Across both repeats:
19,200 fresh trajectories, 6,400 replay draws and 204,800 presentations across the
four trained arms. Training exposure doubles; a higher success rate would not by
itself establish better total-compute efficiency. Record training, generation and
scoring costs separately.

Freshly evaluate six arms: preserved 800-update adapted TB; new 1,600-update
adapted and frozen-head TB; original unchanged supervised head; saved continued
head-only supervised head; and plain ProGen. The saved heads receive no new
supervised training. Use 128 raw outputs per prompt and arm: **1,024 per arm and
repeat, 12,288 total**. Reset the new evaluation seed per arm, without claiming
that different policies generate matched sequences.

Keep every prompt, including RPB1, and every raw output in primary summaries.
Report joint charge/predicted-disorder success, separate charge and disorder
passes, uniqueness, repetition, per-prompt outcomes and inherited fit diagnostics.
V3 remains the disorder criterion; V2 is a related sensitivity check.

Use one common training-tail union per repeat for all six arms: original replay
training tails plus fresh training tails from the **400-, 800- and 1,600-update**
fits. Keeping the historical 400-update exposure preserves the previous copy
boundary. This is deliberately conservative: it is not a claim that each model
trained on every member. Noncopy joint success is accepted outputs absent from
that union divided by **all** raw outputs. Report the union components and retain
child summaries separately, since their own-training definitions differ.

## Decision and stopping rule, fixed before the run

The final 1,600-update adapted model must, **in each repeat**:

1. Gain at least **five percentage points** in raw joint success over its freshly
   sampled preserved 800-update model.
2. Gain at least **five points** in common-union noncopy joint success.
3. Lose no more than **five points** in either unique-tail fraction or V3 disorder
   pass rate against that same control.

Both repeats must pass. These inherited practical thresholds are not statistical
significance tests. Report every difference; means cannot hide a failed repeat.
Only final update-1,600 checkpoints determine the decision. Intermediate samples,
losses and checkpoints are diagnostics, not a basis for selecting a favorable
checkpoint. The inherited comparison against frozen/supervised arms is secondary.

If both pass, retain 1,600 for this task and discuss the next scientific comparison.
Otherwise retain 800 and stop extending the budget. **Neither outcome authorizes
3,200 updates, extra seeds, a reward change or a new property.** Charge/disorder
proxy gains do not establish exact target-distribution matching, new-prompt
generalization, biological function, external-database novelty or an advantage
over a capacity-matched non-GFN model.

are indeed at that path, and `v2` is absent. The filesystem action that made `v1`
reusable was not observed. The earlier failed state is historical, not the current

starts a fresh fit rather than resuming at update 100.
The interruption is an infrastructure failure, not a negative scientific result;

all six arms and all eight prompts. Each percentage below uses 1,024 raw outputs
per arm and repeat. No interim checkpoint was selected.

| Policy | Repeat 1 joint success | Repeat 2 joint success | Mean |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Plain ProGen | 3.22% (33) | 1.76% (18) | 2.49% |

### Decision and quality

| 800 → 1,600 comparison | Repeat 1 | Repeat 2 |
|---|---:|---:|
| Raw joint gain | +4.79 percentage points | +1.66 points |
| Common-union noncopy joint gain | +4.79 points | +1.66 points |
| Charge pass | 33.50% → 37.89% | 32.62% → 35.94% |
| V3 disorder pass | 90.04% → 91.41% | 92.68% → 90.72% |
| Unique-tail fraction | 98.14% → 98.93% | 98.24% → 98.34% |
| Predefined budget screen | **FAIL** | **FAIL** |

Quality checks pass; the failure is insufficient improvement under the fixed
five-point raw/noncopy threshold. With 1,024 outputs, at least 52 additional
successes were needed per repeat; observed gains are 49 and 17. Do not lower the
threshold after seeing the near miss in repeat 1 or treat this engineering gate
as a significance test.

Mean success improves **3.22 percentage points**, or 66 more successes among
2,048 generated outputs (668 versus 602), approximately 10.96% more per raw output.
This is a smaller marginal gain than the preceding 400→800 comparison. It supports
diminishing returns from the doubled schedule, not proof that learning stopped
or that 32.62% is a hard ceiling. Training exposure doubles; this is not a
compute-matched test or proof of total-cost efficiency.

The adapted models retain mean predicted-disorder pass of **91.06%** and mean
within-repeat uniqueness of **98.63%**. None of the 668 successes is an exact
copy of the common original/400/800/1,600 training union. Charge remains the main
limitation: mean charge pass is only 36.91%. Prompt-level joint success increases
on all eight prompts in repeat 1 and five of eight in repeat 2. RPB1 remains the
weakest prompt at 15/128 and 11/128 successes.

### Post-hoc late-learning check

The [independent audit](../verification/idr-lastblock-1600-independent-2026-11-18.json)
also counts fresh adapted-policy trajectories once per refresh, excluding frozen,
prior and replay paths. Each 200-update window contains 400 sampled trajectories.

| Update window | Repeat 1 joint success | Repeat 2 joint success |
|---|---:|---:|
| 801–1,000 | 26.75% | 29.00% |
| 1,001–1,200 | 30.50% | 26.75% |
| 1,201–1,400 | 34.25% | 32.00% |
| 1,401–1,600 | 35.75% | 30.75% |

The final windows disagree: repeat 1's pass rate rises while its mean violation
worsens (0.936→0.995); repeat 2's pass rate eases while mean violation improves
(1.018→1.001). These are samples from evolving training policies, not independent
evaluations of fixed checkpoints. They neither prove convergence nor justify
forecasting another gain from longer training. The final-checkpoint gate remains
the decision rule; these post-hoc diagnostics do not override it.

The five-point rule is a practical adoption threshold, not a convergence detector.
Neither a precise plateau step nor an optimal stopping point has been established.
Both fits retain checkpoints at updates 1,000 and 1,200, but these received no
separate fresh-generation evaluation. If locating a cheaper stopping point becomes
the selected question, evaluate those existing checkpoints under a common fresh
evaluation protocol before considering new training. This would be post-hoc
development analysis, not a replacement for the completed final-checkpoint test.

Those intermediate weights belong to the 1,600-update schedule, whose rates drop
only after 1,200. Separate 1,000/1,200-budget fits would decay after 750/900 under
the inherited convention, so they are different interventions. No such fits or
checkpoint-evaluation run is selected or implemented. More updates could still
help, but the observed gains do not justify another automatic doubling or a step
sweep. The trained 1,600-update models remain usable at the same architecture and
nominal per-sequence generation cost; the initial recommendation to retain 800
does not erase the longer models' higher observed success.

### Interpretation and next decision

**The predeclared rule initially led to a recommendation to retain 800 updates.**
The user subsequently chose to keep the higher-scoring 1,600-update models and
budget, deferring intermediate-checkpoint evaluations and further budget tests.
This is a practical working-budget choice, not a revision of the gate or evidence
of convergence. No new training is needed to retain the completed models.
The next proposed experiment is
supervised fine-tuning of the same final block and guidance head, with equivalent
access to scored training data. The existing head-only supervised comparator does
not isolate the benefit of the GFN objective. A contrasting charge profile remains
a subsequent proposed test of whether the recipe learns another requested pattern.
Neither comparison has been implemented or run on the basis of this result.

The project has a working GFN IDR generator on this development task. This result
does not establish arbitrary property control, unseen-prefix generalization,
experimental disorder, biological function, global target-distribution accuracy
or novelty against external protein databases. The
[benchmark roadmap](../../literature/IDR_BENCHMARK_ROADMAP_2026-11-17.md) owns the
broader comparator and functional-task proposals.
